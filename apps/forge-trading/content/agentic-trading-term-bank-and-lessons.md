# Agentic Trading — Term Bank and Lesson List (Draft 1)

**Purpose:** seed content for the BIGHEAVYINK agentic-trading education vertical. Term bank targets 120 terms across seven sections, matching the Forge format. Lesson list covers both tracks.

**Positioning:** education and tooling only. No individualized recommendations, no account management, no performance claims. Paper trading in all exercises.

---

## Part 1 — Term Bank (120 terms)

### A. Market structure and orders (18)

1. **Bid / ask** — highest price a buyer will pay / lowest a seller will accept; the gap is the spread.
2. **Spread** — bid–ask difference; a direct trading cost, wider in thin names and after hours.
3. **Market order** — executes immediately at the best available price; guarantees fill, not price.
4. **Limit order** — executes only at a stated price or better; guarantees price, not fill.
5. **Stop order** — becomes a market order once a trigger price trades; used to exit losers.
6. **Stop-limit order** — becomes a limit order at trigger; can fail to fill in a fast move.
7. **Trailing stop** — stop that moves with price by a fixed amount or percent, locking in gains.
8. **Time in force** — how long an order lives: day, good-till-canceled (GTC), immediate-or-cancel (IOC), fill-or-kill (FOK).
9. **Extended hours** — pre-market and after-hours sessions; thinner liquidity, wider spreads, limit orders only on most platforms.
10. **Liquidity** — ease of trading size without moving price; measured by volume and depth.
11. **Level 2 / order book** — visible resting bids and asks at each price level.
12. **Slippage** — difference between expected and actual fill price.
13. **Partial fill** — order executed for fewer shares than requested.
14. **Lot** — round lot is 100 shares; odd lots are anything smaller.
15. **Settlement (T+1)** — trade date plus one business day before cash and shares officially change hands.
16. **Volume** — shares traded in a period; confirms or contradicts price moves.
17. **Gap** — price opens away from the prior close; gap up / gap down.
18. **Halt** — exchange pause in trading, usually for news or volatility.

### B. Technical indicators (20)

19. **OHLC** — open, high, low, close; the four prices that make a candle or bar.
20. **Candlestick** — chart bar showing OHLC with a body and wicks; color indicates direction.
21. **Support** — price level where buying has repeatedly stopped declines.
22. **Resistance** — price level where selling has repeatedly stopped advances.
23. **Trend** — sequence of higher highs and higher lows (uptrend) or the reverse.
24. **Simple moving average (SMA)** — arithmetic mean of the last N closes.
25. **Exponential moving average (EMA)** — weighted mean that reacts faster to recent prices.
26. **Golden cross / death cross** — 50-day SMA crossing above / below the 200-day.
27. **RSI (relative strength index)** — 0–100 momentum oscillator; conventionally under 30 oversold, over 70 overbought.
28. **MACD** — difference between 12- and 26-period EMAs plus a 9-period signal line; crossovers flag momentum shifts.
29. **Bollinger Bands** — SMA with bands at ±2 standard deviations; width tracks volatility.
30. **ATR (average true range)** — average of recent bar ranges; a volatility measure used to size stops.
31. **VWAP** — volume-weighted average price for the session; institutional benchmark.
32. **Stochastic oscillator** — position of the close within the recent high–low range.
33. **Divergence** — price makes a new high or low that the indicator does not confirm.
34. **Breakout** — price moves through support or resistance on rising volume.
35. **Mean reversion** — assumption that stretched prices return toward an average.
36. **Momentum** — assumption that recent direction persists.
37. **Overbought / oversold** — indicator readings suggesting a move is stretched; not a signal by itself.
38. **Lookback period** — number of bars an indicator uses; the main tuning knob.

### C. Fundamentals and valuation (15)

39. **Market capitalization** — share price × shares outstanding.
40. **EPS (earnings per share)** — net income ÷ shares outstanding.
41. **P/E ratio** — price ÷ EPS; trailing uses the last four quarters, forward uses estimates.
42. **PEG ratio** — P/E ÷ expected earnings growth rate.
43. **Revenue** — top-line sales before any costs.
44. **Gross, operating, net margin** — profit at each stage as a percent of revenue.
45. **Free cash flow** — operating cash flow minus capital expenditures.
46. **Debt-to-equity** — total liabilities ÷ shareholder equity; leverage measure.
47. **Dividend yield** — annual dividend ÷ share price.
48. **Ex-dividend date** — first day a buyer no longer receives the declared dividend.
49. **Earnings call / earnings date** — quarterly results release; the highest-volatility scheduled event for a stock.
50. **Guidance** — management's forward projections; misses drive most post-earnings moves.
51. **10-K / 10-Q / 8-K** — annual report, quarterly report, material-event filing.
52. **Insider transaction (Form 4)** — officer or director buys and sells, filed within two business days.
53. **Analyst consensus** — average of published estimates and price targets.

### D. Risk and position management (20)

54. **Position size** — dollars or shares committed to one trade.
55. **Risk per trade** — amount you accept losing if the stop is hit; commonly 0.5–2% of account.
56. **Stop distance** — entry price minus stop price; combined with risk per trade, it determines size.
57. **Risk-reward ratio** — potential gain ÷ potential loss at the planned exit points.
58. **Expectancy** — (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss); the only number that says whether a rule makes money.
59. **Win rate** — percent of trades closed at a profit; meaningless without average win and loss.
60. **Drawdown** — peak-to-trough decline in account value.
61. **Max drawdown** — the largest drawdown over a period; the pain metric.
62. **Round trip** — a completed buy-and-sell (or sell-and-cover) pair.
63. **Realized vs. unrealized P&L** — closed profit or loss vs. open mark-to-market.
64. **Scaling in / out** — entering or exiting in tranches instead of all at once.
65. **Correlation** — degree to which positions move together; concentrated correlation is hidden size.
66. **Diversification** — spreading exposure across uncorrelated positions.
67. **Leverage / margin** — borrowing to increase position size; amplifies both directions.
68. **Margin call** — broker demand for cash when equity falls below the maintenance requirement.
69. **Pattern day trader rule** — four or more day trades in five business days in a margin account triggers a $25,000 equity minimum.
70. **Buying power** — cash plus available margin.
71. **Kelly criterion** — formula for the bet size that maximizes long-run growth; usually applied at a fraction.
72. **Trade journal** — record of thesis, entry, exit, size, and outcome; the raw material for every review.
73. **Post-mortem** — structured review of a closed trade against its original thesis.

### E. Tax and accounting (10)

74. **Cost basis** — purchase price plus commissions; the starting point for gain calculations.
75. **Tax lot** — a specific purchase batch with its own basis and date.
76. **Lot selection (FIFO, LIFO, specific ID)** — method for choosing which lot a sale closes; changes the taxable gain.
77. **Short-term vs. long-term gain** — held one year or less vs. more than one year; taxed at different rates.
78. **Wash sale** — selling at a loss and rebuying substantially identical shares within 30 days before or after; the loss is deferred.
79. **Capital loss carryforward** — net losses beyond the annual deduction limit carry to future years.
80. **1099-B** — broker's annual report of sales and basis.
81. **Qualified dividend** — dividend taxed at long-term rates if holding-period rules are met.
82. **Mark-to-market** — valuing open positions at current price; also an election available to trader-status filers.
83. **Trader tax status** — IRS classification for high-frequency, full-time traders; hard to qualify for.

### F. Regulation and compliance (14)

84. **SEC** — federal regulator of securities markets and issuers.
85. **FINRA** — self-regulatory body for broker-dealers and registered representatives.
86. **Broker-dealer** — firm that executes trades for customers or its own account; registered with SEC and FINRA.
87. **Registered investment adviser (RIA)** — firm or person paid to give individualized investment advice; registered with SEC or a state.
88. **Fiduciary duty** — obligation to act in the client's best interest; applies to RIAs.
89. **Publisher's exclusion** — Advisers Act carve-out for impersonal, general, regularly circulated publications; the line an education product must stay behind.
90. **Investment advice** — recommendations tailored to a specific person's situation; the trigger for RIA registration.
91. **Regulation Best Interest** — standard broker-dealers owe retail customers when recommending securities.
92. **Insider trading** — trading on material non-public information; illegal regardless of how it was obtained.
93. **Market manipulation** — pump-and-dump, spoofing, wash trading; illegal.
94. **Accredited investor** — income or net-worth threshold that unlocks private offerings.
95. **Suitability** — older broker standard: recommendation must fit the customer's profile.
96. **SIPC** — insures brokerage accounts against firm failure, not against losses.
97. **Disclaimer / disclosure** — required plain-language statements that content is educational and not advice.

### G. Agent and automation concepts (23)

98. **Agent** — LLM plus tools plus a loop that observes, reasons, acts, and repeats.
99. **Tool call** — structured request from the model to an external function (get quote, place order).
100. **MCP (Model Context Protocol)** — open standard for exposing tools to a model; Robinhood and others ship MCP servers.
101. **Brokerage API** — programmatic interface for quotes, orders, and positions (Robinhood, Alpaca, Interactive Brokers).
102. **Paper trading** — simulated account with real prices and fake money; the only environment for learning.
103. **Human-in-the-loop** — every consequential action waits for explicit human approval.
104. **Approval gate** — the specific checkpoint where an order is proposed but not sent.
105. **Preview / review order** — dry-run API call returning cost, fees, and buying-power impact without executing.
106. **Watchlist rule** — condition the agent evaluates on a schedule (e.g., RSI below a threshold).
107. **Trigger** — condition that fires a rule; price level, indicator value, time, or news.
108. **Alert** — notification that a trigger fired; the agent's lowest-risk output.
109. **Scheduler / cron** — mechanism that runs the agent at fixed intervals.
110. **System prompt** — standing instructions defining the agent's role, limits, and forbidden actions.
111. **Structured output** — model response constrained to a schema (JSON) so code can act on it.
112. **Grammar / schema constraint** — technique forcing the model to emit only valid structured output.
113. **Guardrail** — hard-coded check outside the model: max position size, allowed symbols, order-type whitelist.
114. **Idempotency** — designing actions so a retry does not place a duplicate order.
115. **Audit log** — immutable record of every observation, reasoning step, proposal, and approval.
116. **Hallucination** — model states a price, fill, or position that does not exist; why every fact must come from a tool result.
117. **Confirmation bias (in agents)** — model rationalizes a trade it already leaned toward; countered by requiring a stated invalidation condition.
118. **Backtest** — running a rule against historical data; useful, and easy to overfit.
119. **Overfitting** — rule tuned so tightly to past data that it fails on new data.
120. **Kill switch** — single action that halts the agent and cancels open orders.

## Part 2 — Lesson List

### Track 1 — Market Literacy (10 modules, term-and-scenario format)

1. **How a trade happens** — orders, spreads, fills, settlement. Terms A.
2. **Reading a chart** — candles, trend, support and resistance. Terms B.19–23.
3. **Indicators that matter** — moving averages, RSI, MACD, ATR, and what each does not tell you. Terms B.24–38.
4. **What a company is worth** — filings, earnings, valuation ratios. Terms C.
5. **Sizing the trade** — risk per trade, stop distance, position size math. Terms D.54–57.
6. **Does the rule make money?** — expectancy, win rate, drawdown, journaling. Terms D.58–73.
7. **Margin and its rules** — leverage, calls, PDT. Terms D.67–70.
8. **Taxes on trades** — lots, wash sales, 1099-B. Terms E.
9. **Who regulates what** — SEC, FINRA, RIA line, publisher's exclusion. Terms F.
10. **Capstone** — build a written trading plan: universe, rules, size, exits, review cadence.

### Track 2 — Building and Supervising a Trading Agent (12 lessons)

Each lesson has an objective, a build step, and a deliverable. All exercises run on a paper account.

1. **What an agent is and is not**
   Objective: separate "agent" from "autotrader." Deliverable: a one-page spec of what your agent may observe, propose, and never do.

2. **Set up the sandbox**
   Objective: get a paper account and API keys (Alpaca free tier). Deliverable: a script that pulls a quote and prints positions.

3. **The observe step — quotes and indicators**
   Objective: fetch OHLCV bars and compute RSI and a moving average in code. Deliverable: a function returning a symbol snapshot as JSON.

4. **The watchlist rule**
   Objective: express a rule in plain language and in code ("flag CHRW when RSI < 35 and price above 200-day"). Deliverable: a rule file the agent loads at startup.

5. **The system prompt and structured output**
   Objective: write the agent's standing instructions and force it to answer in a fixed schema (symbol, action, size, reasoning, invalidation). Deliverable: prompt plus schema.

6. **Guardrails outside the model**
   Objective: enforce limits the model cannot override — symbol whitelist, max size, order-type restriction, market-hours check. Deliverable: a validation layer that rejects non-compliant proposals.

7. **The approval gate**
   Objective: the agent proposes, previews, and waits. Deliverable: a Slack or email message with the proposal and approve/reject buttons; nothing executes without the button.

8. **Execution and idempotency**
   Objective: place the paper order once, and only once, even if the process retries. Deliverable: order submission with a client order ID and duplicate check.

9. **The audit log and trade journal**
   Objective: record every observation, proposal, decision, and fill. Deliverable: an append-only log and a journal entry per round trip (thesis, entry, exit, outcome, lesson).

10. **Scheduling and the kill switch**
    Objective: run the loop on a timer and be able to stop it in one action. Deliverable: a cron or systemd timer plus a halt command that cancels open orders.

11. **Reviewing the agent's decisions**
    Objective: weekly post-mortem — did the rule fire correctly, did the reasoning match the data, did guardrails catch anything. Deliverable: a review template and one completed review.

12. **Capstone — the CHRW loop, end to end**
    Objective: reproduce a full supervised round trip from watchlist trigger to journal entry on paper. Deliverable: a working agent, its audit log, and a written post-mortem.

### Optional Track 3 — Agent OS integration (for B2B and advanced learners)

- Packaging the trading supervisor as an Agent OS skill (SKILL.md, references, scripts).
- Multi-agent split: observer, proposer, compliance checker, executor.
- Institutional controls: dual approval, role separation, retention of logs.
